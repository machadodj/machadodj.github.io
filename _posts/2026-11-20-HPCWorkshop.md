# A full room for the cluster

On Friday, November 20, 2026, the Charlotte HPC Group ran its Advanced HPC Cluster Training, and the room filled up. Students and researchers packed the tables in Room 408 of the Session Family Building, the building we used to call Bioinformatics. Many of them worked on laptops for the whole hour.

I am the group's faculty advisor, and this workshop made me proud. The attendance and the energy were wild. The credit goes to [Anastasiia Duchenko](https://phyloinformatics.com/members/Anastasiia_Duchenko.html), the group's president, and to the students who run the group with her.

## What the hour covered

The workshop ran four short modules and closed with a hands-on session. The first module covered resource allocation, which means asking the scheduler for the cores, memory, and time a job actually needs. The second module introduced multithreading and multiprocessing, so a job can use more than one core. Sadie Hollenack presented the second module. The third module covered program installation on a cluster where users cannot install software as root. The fourth module covered the Data Transfer Node, the right way to move large files on and off the system.

In the hands-on session, participants ran live code examples on the cluster. They worked on Starlight, the research cluster that the Office of OneIT runs for UNC Charlotte.

## Why this group matters

Most of the work in our lab runs on a cluster, and the first semester on a cluster is hard. Nobody is born knowing how to write a job script or how many cores to request. The Charlotte HPC Group exists so that students learn these habits from each other. Members get peer help with job scripts, environment setup, and pipeline debugging. Members also write training materials and lead workshops like this one.

The group is open to everyone, from people who have never submitted a cluster job to people who already tune parallel code. Faculty, staff, and campus affiliates are welcome too. Newcomers can start with the free [HPC Cluster Training](https://instructure.charlotte.edu/courses/207684) course on Canvas. Any active UNC Charlotte student, faculty member, or staff member can self-enroll.
