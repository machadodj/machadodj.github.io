# Thankful for Vladimir

Today is Thanksgiving, and our lab has a long list of people to thank. This post goes to one of them. [Dr. Vladimir Mashanov](https://phyloinformatics.com/members/Vladimir_Mashanov.html) is a developmental and cell biologist at the Wake Forest Institute for Regenerative Medicine. He is also a collaborator of our lab and a friend.

## An expert on echinoderms

Vladimir studies echinoderms, the group that includes sea stars, sea cucumbers, and brittle stars. He asks how these animals renew and regrow their tissues, including the nervous system. His toolkit runs from histology and electron microscopy to confocal imaging, cell culture, and RNA-seq. Few people know both the animals and the molecular methods this well.

That knowledge shows in our papers. In 2023, Vladimir led our review of [radial glia in echinoderm cell renewal, growth, and regeneration](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2023.1258370). In 2024, he co-authored [our study of mutable collagenous tissue in the brittle star *Ophiomastix wendtii*](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-024-10926-7). That tissue lets a brittle star stiffen or soften its ligaments on command.

## Help that reaches our students

Our students who work on echinoderms in the wet lab feel Vladimir's help the most. When Reyhaneh Nouri and I set up in situ hybridization for her doctoral research, the open questions were practical ones. Which PCR product needs purification before we make a riboprobe? How long does a probe keep in the freezer? Is an old cDNA stock still safe to use? Vladimir answered each question in a few lines. Store riboprobes at -80 C, he told us, and make fresh cDNA rather than trust an old stock. Answers like these keep a student from losing weeks at the bench.

Vladimir runs a busy research program of his own, so his help reaches us in small, well-aimed doses: a quick reply to a protocol question, a note on a draft, or a pointer to the right paper. We are careful with his time, and he is generous with what he knows. That combination has made our students better and more confident scientists.

## Thank you

Thank you, Vladimir, for your expertise, your patience with our questions, and your friendship. Happy Thanksgiving from all of us in the Phyloinformatics Lab.
